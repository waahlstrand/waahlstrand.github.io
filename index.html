I work on explainable deep learning for medical image analysis. My main application is predicting osteoporotic fracture risk from DXA, spine radiographs and HR-pQCT, with models whose predictions can be traced back to specific vertebrae, tissues and clinical criteria.

I came to medical imaging from machine learning for materials and microscopy, and I also contribute computational methods to projects in digital humanities.

    Vertebral fractures identified on lateral DXA images by deep learning predict incident fractures in older women

    Mattias Lorentzon, Victor Wåhlstrand, Jennifer Alvén, Ida Häggström, Lisa Johansson, et al.

    In a cohort of 423 women aged 75–80 followed for about eight years, vertebral fractures found automatically by XVFA predicted incident fractures as well as manual assessment did.

  2. 2025 MLMI workshop, MICCAI

    Patch-level attribution of multimodal fracture risk prediction

    Victor Wåhlstrand, Erik Blomqvist, Jennifer Alvén, Lisa Johansson, Mattias Lorentzon, Ida Häggström

    An annotation-free multimodal model that predicts fracture risk from low-dose spine X-rays and attributes the risk to individual vertebrae. Adding vertebral-body patches clearly improves C-index and Brier score over clinical baselines.

    Paper
  3. 2025 MICCAI

    Separable tissue representations for attributable risk prediction

    Victor Wåhlstrand, Jennifer Alvén, Lisa Johansson, Kristian Axelsson, Mattias Lorentzon, Ida Häggström

    STRAP, a masked autoencoder over a variable number of patch tokens, lets bone and soft tissue in HR-pQCT enter a fracture risk model separately. Including soft tissue measurably improves prediction.

    Paper

    Sticks and stones may break your bones: Explainable methods for fracture risk prediction

    Victor Wåhlstrand

    Explainable methods for detecting vertebral fractures and forecasting future fractures from DXA and HR-pQCT. They perform on par with or better than black-box alternatives.

    Thesis

    Victor Wåhlstrand Skärström, Lisa Johansson, Jennifer Alvén, Mattias Lorentzon, Ida Häggström
