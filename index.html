I specialize in explainable survival analysis in medical image analysis, with a current focus on predicting osteoporotic fracture risk from DXA, spinal radiographs and HR-pQCT, with models whose predictions can be traced back to specific vertebrae, tissues and clinical criteria. Moreover, I am interested in facets of explainability, such as attribution, uncertainty, causality and fairness, and how (and if) these can be integrated into clinical decision support systems.

Looking at my research profile may be a bit confusing; I have a background in physics and complex adaptive systems, and have published on microscopy imaging and simulation of materials, but I also have experience as a research assistant in digital humanities, working with images of rock carvings and historical documents. Since then, I have turned to medical image analysis, and I am currently a doctoral student at Chalmers University of Technology, Sweden.
